Glasgow eight-pole deflection design

Reuse the upstream differential X/Y scan signals. Generate four opposite-pole drives with controlled pair averages to reproduce all eight electrode voltages for each deflector.

The source assumption is X+, X−, Y+, Y−, which forms two differential analog pairs. This matches the reference OBI revA4 M8 connector. If the installed upstream hardware exposes four complete differential pairs, identify each by stepping X, Y and the correction controls separately. Reuse extra pairs for Va and Vb only if their independent control, range and timing are verified.

Use the installed separator and SMH drive if they can reproduce the complete voltage matrix below. For a new low-voltage separator, recover X and Y with differential receivers, apply calibrated gains and offsets, then generate each pair’s half-difference and average. The approved deflector drive stage must reproduce both quantities at the electrodes. Deflector 2 is the master. Deflector 1 takes a signed factor k on the complete master voltage vector and requires its own eight physical electrode drives when both deflectors are fitted.

Design choice. Keep the pixel-rate matrix in analog hardware driven by the existing X/Y DACs. Spare Glasgow pins can control a separate low-speed DAC for offsets and stigmation. Pixel-dependent correction requires independently commanded, synchronous analog channels with bandwidth and settling verified at the required scan rate.

Eight electrode voltages

Use the design documents’ coefficient a = 0.414 and inputs x = Vx + Vox and y = Vy + Voy. Calibrate the differential OBI output to the Vx and Vy units of the plate equations. Each row below pairs opposite electrodes in the source document’s numbering.

The terminal-to-terminal voltage of each pair is 2h. The pair average is m. Applying Va or Vb shifts both electrodes of each affected pair together, with equal and opposite shifts on the other affected pair. A conventional fixed-average differential driver can reproduce the X/Y differences. The stigmation terms additionally need controlled pair averages at the electrodes. For the slave, scale both h and m by k. Differential output and common-mode control

For each pair, the largest absolute electrode voltage is |m| + |h|. Use that value and its |k|-scaled equivalent to check headroom on both deflectors. The supply documents’ 200 V generator figure and the SMH sheet’s ±150 V rails describe different points in the chain. Set command limits using the measured usable plate swing, gain, load, offset and common-mode range. If a scan exceeds that envelope, reduce its amplitude coherently to preserve the plate ratios.

Use the J18 rows if the existing separator is bypassed. Start by tracing numbered electrodes to actual SMH plate letters and verifying signal polarity. The supplied SMH sheet labels a single A–H plate set. Its X1/Y1 and X2/Y2 outputs alone do not establish a second eight-electrode drive path. Keep the J7 plate harness and confirm the second deflector’s driver and harness on the instrument. Use shielded pairs and verify signal returns against the equipment grounding scheme.

SMH compatibility test. At the intended filter and gain setting, measure the eight plate responses to independent X, Y, Va and Vb commands. Identify which installed steering/correction inputs, including J17 paths where applicable, control the pair averages. A differential-only J18 receiver may reject incoming common-mode changes. If the installed arrangement cannot produce all four independent voltage patterns, retain the original correction path or use a qualified drive stage with independently controllable pair averages. A J18 pin assignment by itself leaves this unresolved.

Bench acceptance

  1. Record the installed board revisions and confirm whether the source has two differential pairs or four. Verify the column routing, electrode numbering, polarity, and master/slave connection.
  2. At representative loads, step X alone, Y alone, Va alone and Vb alone. For each test measure all eight electrode responses. Use the expected half-differences and averages to distinguish a wiring or sign error from a missing correction path. Verify the signed slave scaling independently.
  3. Compare requested and measured plate voltages at combined scan corners and maximum correction. Check clipping, settling, and cross-coupling under the intended gain and load. The upstream X and Y latches are separated by two 48 MHz FPGA clocks, about 41.7 ns. Include that skew in fast-transition measurements.
  4. With the beam inhibited under the equipment service procedure, verify blanking, transfer to the new source, recovery to OEM, and response to power or control loss. Release the harness after the measured voltage matrix, grounding, blanking and interlocks are approved.

Commissioning profiles and FPGA lock

The active repository profile runs in coordinate loopback. The commissioning profile selects physical ADC/DAC I/O. Both build with the FPGA takeover lock engaged. Even if a host requests external control or unblanking, the FPGA holds the selector outputs low and asserts blank. On port startup A0, A2 and A4 pull low and A3 pulls high. Release this lock in the intended physical profile only after qualifying the wiring, amplifier transfer and independent hardware permit.

Comparing measured plate voltages

The repository voltage calculator accepts eight measured plate voltages per deflector and an explicit tolerance. Compare individual plate errors, pair-average errors and half-difference errors after each X, Y and stigmation test. The calculator returns a failing exit status when any measured plate exceeds tolerance. The optional correction-DAC pin plan is checked for conflicts. Physical correction outputs require a selected DAC and control interface.
